I'm a [research software engineer](https://researchsoftware.org/) (RSE) on [Genomic Data Science Core](https://www.sidra.org/research/services1/genomic-data-science-core/) at [Sidra Medicine](https://www.sidra.org/), which looks like full-stack development over the anarchy of fragmented <span class="dm-mark"> high-stakes data </span> (part of the work made it into a [poster](/assets/pdf/SARF-2026-Poster.pdf) at [SARF '26](https://www.qatar.cmu.edu/news/cmuq-alum-genomic-data-platform-sidra-forum/)). Genomics is a big-data problem in its own right ([on par with astronomy/social media](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002195)) as sequencing gets accessible, yet more data hasn't made drug discovery any more efficient (if anything, it's gone the other way - [following Moore's law backwards!](https://centuryofbio.com/p/eroom)). Part of that is a tooling problem: life science doesn't have a sustainable ecosystem for software development, so most tools [get built for temporary scope then abandoned](https://newscience.org/how-software-in-the-life-sciences-actually-works-and-doesnt-work/), and/or [aren't installable in a user friendly manner](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000333).

On top of it, life sciences demands [a level of transparency and reproducibility that isn't always straightforward](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1013552). Plus, most of what I touch sits upstream of critical decisions at precision medicine and/or scientific discovery. All of it makes it so much fun compared most corporate SWE gigs ever get to be (let's not even talk about [bullshit jobs](https://strikemag.org/bullshit-jobs/)).

On the side, I explore the wild landscape of open-source tools trying to shape conventional software engineering around the scope of life science. One such attempt is a [desktop app](https://github.com/molgenis/interAGt) for [AlphaGenome](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-ai-for-better-understanding-the-genome/) I'm building with the [molgenis](https://github.com/molgenis) folks at UMCG (a follow-up to my own [attempt](https://github.com/Abrar-Abir/alphagenome-viewer)), about making predictions accessible to non-technical users. Another [scratch work](https://github.com/Abrar-Abir/clawbio2.0) is about trying to make LLM agent workflows <span class="dm-mark">robust and reproducible</span>, chasing the determinism that genomics demands. Also, a weekend detour was [hackathon project](https://fritzsedlazeck.github.io/blog/2026/hackathon-2026/) where I built the [foldback caller](https://github.com/collaborativebioinformatics/Foldback_hunters/) to detect [ONT](https://nanoporetech.com/platform/technology) artifacts that [SV callers misread as real](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40791372/).

Not long ago, I had a good sprint with ML research. An [NLP](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ehn/11-611/) course pulled me into shared tasks ([ArabicNLP '24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01360), [CLEF '24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10252)), and one thing led to another: a [QNRF](http://blogs.qu.edu.qa/cse/tag/qnrf/)-funded [CMUQ](https://www.qatar.cmu.edu/news/carnegie-mellon-receives-grants-for-ai-research-into-key-areas-for-qatar/) &times; [QU](http://qufaculty.qu.edu.qa/telsayed/current_research_projects/) collaboration on automated essay scoring for Arabic produced a new dataset ([EACL '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24235)). Turns out SOTA methods built for Eurocentric languages doesn't really work out-of-the-box outside the language family.

Among [other](https://abrar-abir.github.io/now/) things, I'm (currently) curious about epigenomics &times; ML and agentic genomics. Downtime is [lettering](https://abrar-abir.github.io/art/), [chess](https://abrar-abir.github.io/chess/), or hanging out in the bio/arXiv.

As usual, I'm open to collaborating on over-ambitious projects, discussing nearly-impossible ideas, or connecting over almost-thoughtful conversations.
